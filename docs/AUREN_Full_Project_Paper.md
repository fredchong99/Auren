**Executive Summary**

This paper presents AUREN, an AI-powered rehearsal platform for retail investors, as a submission to the IOSCO TechSprint 2026. The entire argument can be stated in five sentences.

First: retail investors are using AI tools every day without ever being trained to question them — and AI has simultaneously lowered the cost of investment fraud to near-zero. These two developments are not separate trends. They are the same trend viewed from two sides, and they converge inside every retail investor's phone.

Second: existing investor education delivers knowledge through lectures and quizzes, but knowledge does not survive contact with pressure — only skill does, and skill is built through rehearsal. This is the central design insight of AUREN v2 and the reason its architecture differs from every current investor education product.

Third: AUREN is an AI rehearsal platform where retail investors practice, in safe simulation, the exact situations that determine their outcomes — deepfake endorsements, misleading AI tools, social-media pressure, and market panic. A hyper-realistic Digital Human hosts every step of the journey, playing both mentor and adversary in service of the learner's protection.

Fourth: every rehearsal is scored with quote-level evidence, updates a five-dimension competency score, and can be certified. This means AUREN produces measurable behavioral change, not just measurable content consumption — a distinction that matters enormously to the regulators who will judge this TechSprint and, eventually, deploy its winning solutions.

Fifth: AUREN is the rehearsal ground between retail investors and the AI-era information environment they already live in. It is built for cross-jurisdictional deployment by regulators from day one — multilingual, configurable per jurisdiction, and auditable at every layer.

**Part One: The Problem**

**1. The moment we're in**

Artificial intelligence did not arrive at investor education slowly. It arrived everywhere, simultaneously — and it arrived on two fronts at once.

On the user side, retail investors now ask AI tools how to invest, what to buy, when to sell, what scams look like, and whether sources are credible. They treat AI responses as authoritative. Chat interfaces have become, for millions of people, the first and often the only research step before a financial decision. Yet most retail investors have never been taught to verify an AI output, to cross-check a confident claim, or to distinguish between AI-generated education and AI-generated advice. The tools arrived years before the literacy needed to use them safely.

On the threat side, generative AI has lowered the cost of producing high-fidelity deepfakes, synthetic identities, voice clones, and fabricated investment communications to near-zero. What once required a skilled fraud operation can now be produced by a single actor with consumer-grade tools: fraud at scale, personalized to each victim, indistinguishable from legitimate sources, and distributed at social-media velocity.

These two forces meet inside every retail investor's phone. The question facing regulators is no longer whether to teach AI literacy. The question is how fast — and, critically, whether learners can practice these skills rather than merely study them.

**2. The scale of AI-enabled fraud**

The threat environment is evolving faster than existing safeguards, and the available data understates the problem because fraud is systematically under-reported.

The FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center reported $12.5 billion in digital fraud losses in 2023 — a 22 percent year-on-year increase. Within that total, investment fraud was the costliest single category at $4.57 billion, up 38 percent year-on-year. Meanwhile, identity-verification providers have documented an approximately tenfold increase in global deepfake incidents between 2022 and 2023, with some providers reporting increases measured in the thousands of percent over three-year windows.

What changed is not simply the volume of fraud but its production model. Fraud is no longer a craft. It is a manufactured product. Generative AI compresses the time-to-fraud from weeks to minutes, the cost from thousands of dollars to cents, and the believability from "obvious" to "indistinguishable." A single bad actor can now run thousands of personalized, multilingual scams in parallel — each one tuned to the demographic, language, and emotional vulnerability of its target.

For the retail investor, this represents a structural shift in the risk landscape. The traditional heuristics — poor spelling, generic greetings, implausible claims — no longer identify fraudulent content, because AI produces fraudulent content with none of those tells. The defense must shift from spotting surface errors to recognizing behavioral patterns. That shift requires training. Training of a kind that currently does not exist at retail scale.

**3. Why retail investors are uniquely exposed**

Five compounding vulnerabilities make retail investors the most exposed population in this new threat environment — and each one is amplified, not merely exploited, by AI.

The first is information asymmetry, widened by AI. Generative AI floods the information space with content that mimics authentic financial communication: fabricated research reports, synthetic news articles, cloned analyst commentary. Retail investors lack the tools and the training to distinguish them from the genuine article, and the volume of synthetic content is growing faster than any verification infrastructure.

The second is social media as the primary vector. Investment decisions are increasingly mediated by TikTok, WhatsApp, Telegram, and Discord — environments optimized for virality rather than verification, and structurally ripe for AI-driven impersonation. When a retail investor's primary information diet comes from platforms with no editorial layer, the burden of verification falls entirely on the individual.

The third is behavioral vulnerability targeted at scale. AI scams exploit trust in familiar figures, emotional resonance, and perceived urgency — the same biases human fraudsters have always exploited. The difference is deliverability: what a skilled con artist once did to one victim at a time, AI now does to millions in minutes, with each attempt automatically tuned to its target.

The fourth is overreliance on AI-generated outputs. Many investors treat AI responses as authoritative analysis, even when those responses are built on incomplete data, outdated information, or correlation-based reasoning disconnected from market fundamentals. The interface conveys confidence; the underlying epistemics often do not warrant it.

The fifth is hallucination presented with certainty. Generative AI systems produce confident-sounding but false statements — fabricated facts, invented figures, non-existent citations. In a financial context, a hallucinated statistic delivered with the fluency of a trusted advisor can drive a real loss. The learner who cannot recognize the signature of a hallucination has no defense against it.

**4. Why existing investor education fails**

Existing investor education was built for a pre-AI world, and its inadequacy is structural rather than incremental. The dominant model across jurisdictions is knowledge delivery: static content in the form of text, videos, and PDFs; generic material that is identical for every learner; assessment that tests memorization rather than judgment; feedback that arrives after the module ends rather than during the moment of decision; and certification that attests to course completion rather than demonstrated skill.

Underneath these design choices sits a shared assumption: that the moment of pressure will not come, or that if it comes, recalled knowledge will be sufficient. The evidence says otherwise. Investors who can correctly define loss aversion in a quiz still panic-sell in a crash. Investors who have read about romance scams still transfer funds to persuasive strangers. Investors who know, abstractly, that deepfakes exist still trust the video of a familiar CEO. Knowledge does not survive contact with pressure.

What the AI era demands is the opposite architecture at every point: live rehearsal instead of passive consumption; adaptive paths instead of generic content; measurement of judgment under pressure instead of recall under calm; feedback delivered live, during the rehearsal, at the moment the mistake is being made; certification of demonstrated skill, evidenced at the level of the learner's own words; and explicit, sustained treatment of AI-specific risks — hallucinations, deepfakes, synthetic personas — rather than silence about them.

The gap between those two columns is the opportunity AUREN exists to fill. Skill is built through rehearsal. AUREN v2 delivers skill.

**Part Two: The Solution**

**5. Introducing AUREN**

AUREN is an AI rehearsal platform for retail investors, hosted by a hyper-realistic Digital Human. It puts learners inside the situations that will define their real-world outcomes: a misleading AI chatbot delivering confident but fabricated investment claims; a deepfake video of a trusted CEO endorsing a suspicious platform; a WhatsApp contact pressuring them to invest before an artificial deadline; a market panic tempting them to abandon their plan and sell everything. Each of these is delivered as a live rehearsal — not a lesson.

The Digital Human hosts every step of the journey. She conducts the onboarding interview. She plays adversarial personas during rehearsals — the scam recruiter, the pressuring friend, the urgent caller. She provides live coaching mid-conversation, intervening at the moment a learner uses risky phrasing or skips a verification step. And she delivers evidential scorecards afterwards: what the learner said, when they said it, why it mattered, and what the alternative response would have been.

Every rehearsal updates a five-dimension competency score. Every certification is earned through demonstrated skill, not granted for completed modules. Every session teaches the learner what to do — in the moment, when it counts. This is the difference between an academy and a rehearsal ground, and it is the difference that defines AUREN v2.

**6. Four capabilities, one integrated platform**

AUREN combines four operating capabilities. None of them exist together in any current investor education product, and their integration — rather than any single component — is what makes the platform distinctive.

The first capability is the hyper-realistic Digital Human. She is the AI host present at every step: conducting interviews, playing adversarial personas in rehearsals, coaching live, and delivering scorecards. She is not a mascot or a chat widget. She is the mentor every retail investor would have if they could afford a full-time financial educator — attentive, adaptive, and always available.

The second capability is the Rehearsal Engine, the core learning mechanic of the platform. Its operating pattern is: scenario, then AI persona, then live conversation, then live coaching, then evidential scorecard. Learners rehearse deepfake encounters, AI-tool dialogues, social-pressure moments, and market-panic scenarios in safe simulation. The engine is content-agnostic: new scenarios, new personas, and new jurisdictions plug into the same machinery without platform changes.

The third capability is the AILS Cognitive Assessment — a five-dimension behavioral analysis covering Investor Reasoning, AI Literacy, Risk Awareness, Scam Resistance, and Behavioral Stability. The score is generated at onboarding through a conversational interview, and then — critically — it updates continuously. Every rehearsal contributes evidence. AILS is not a snapshot. It is a trajectory.

The fourth capability is the certification and dashboard layer. Rehearsal sessions are automatically classified as certified, needs-review, or requires-retraining. Individual learners track their progress across competencies. Institutions — banks, universities, employers — see the aggregate readiness of their populations. Regulators see anonymized cohort analytics and vulnerability heatmaps across their jurisdictions. The same underlying data serves three distinct governance needs without duplication.

**7. Architecture: four interoperable layers**

AUREN is structured as four interoperable layers, each independently configurable for the regulatory and linguistic context of a deploying jurisdiction.

The Interaction Layer contains the Digital Human, multilingual voice, live coaching delivery, and real-time adaptation. This is the layer the learner experiences directly — the face, voice, and conversational presence of the platform.

The Intelligence Layer contains the rehearsal engine, the AI persona library, AILS cognitive scoring, and the evidential scorecard generator. This is where learner behavior is analyzed, scored, and converted into both feedback and competency data.

The Trust Layer contains integrated eKYC with liveness detection, QR-to-phone verification, ID and passport scanning, privacy-by-design data handling, and audit logs. This layer makes the credential trustworthy and the platform deployable in regulated environments.

The Deployment Layer spans web, mobile, kiosk, embedded widget, and a regulator API. The same platform serves a consumer on a phone, a bank customer at a branch kiosk, a student inside a university portal, and a regulator's analytics team through a governed API.

Because each layer is independently configurable — language, jurisdiction, compliance regime, deployment surface — a single AUREN build can serve any IOSCO member jurisdiction without bespoke rebuilds. Configuration, not code changes, is the localization mechanism.

**Part Three: Answering the IOSCO Problem Statements**

**8. Problem Statement 1, part one: rehearsing deepfake and voice-clone encounters**

IOSCO's first problem statement asks: how can technology help retail investors build skills and knowledge so that they are able to better identify and avoid AI-enabled fraud and scams, including deepfakes on social media, with this technology being accessible across multiple jurisdictions and cultural contexts?

AUREN's answer begins with rehearsal of the two most technically sophisticated fraud vectors: deepfake video and voice cloning.

In a deepfake video rehearsal, the learner watches a fabricated video of a well-known figure endorsing a fraudulent investment — for example, a technology CEO promising guaranteed thirty percent monthly returns on a crypto platform. AUREN pauses the video mid-play and asks, live: what would you do next? The learner's response is captured in their own words, coached in real time, and scored against verification competencies. Along the way, AUREN teaches the detection signals — lip-sync mismatch, unnatural blink rate, audio compression artifacts, lighting inconsistency — not as a lecture, but as annotations on the exact video the learner is evaluating.

In a voice clone rehearsal, an AI persona calls the learner in a cloned voice, urgently requesting a fund transfer due to a fabricated security issue. The learner conducts the conversation live. AUREN coaches mid-call: is this the right verification protocol? Should you act on urgency? What happens if you insist on calling back through a verified channel? Every hesitation, every acquiescence, and every push-back is captured and scored. The learner experiences — safely — the exact emotional pressure a real voice-clone scam applies, and builds the response pattern before the real call ever comes.

**9. Problem Statement 1, part two: rehearsing social-media pressure and synthetic personas**

Most retail investors encounter scams on the platforms where they spend the most time, and the dynamics of those platforms — group pressure, social proof, trusted intermediaries — are precisely what AI-enabled fraud exploits. AUREN rehearses those dynamics directly.

In a social-media pressure rehearsal, the learner is added to an AI-simulated "private investment group." Personas within the group pressure them to invest, share fabricated profit screenshots, invoke urgency, and question their commitment when they hesitate. This mirrors the actual recruitment dynamics of WhatsApp investment groups, Telegram pump-and-dump rings, and Discord trading communities. AUREN coaches live and scores every response — resistance, engagement, or capitulation — because the difference between those three responses is the difference between a protected investor and a victim.

In a synthetic-finfluencer rehearsal, the learner is shown investment advice from an influencer account — and rehearses the verification workflow live: reverse image search, regulator-registry lookup, credentials check, content-pattern audit. Some influencer accounts giving financial advice are AI-generated personas that do not correspond to any real person. The defense AUREN teaches is not perfect detection, which is increasingly impossible, but a behavioral rule rehearsed until it becomes instinct: never act on advice from anyone you cannot verify.

Rehearsal libraries are regionalized. UK FCA warnings, Monetary Authority of Singapore typologies, SEBI alerts, and CNMV advisories each feed jurisdiction-specific scenario sets, continuously updated by partner regulators. A learner in Kuala Lumpur rehearses the scams circulating in Malaysia; a learner in Madrid rehearses the ones circulating in Spain.

**10. Problem Statement 2, part one: rehearsing conversations with AI tools**

IOSCO's second problem statement asks: how can technology be leveraged to educate retail investors about using AI as a tool to learn about investing in capital markets, empowering confident choices, while helping them understand key risks such as AI hallucinations, lack of explainability, and lack of transparency?

AUREN's answer treats the learner's relationship with AI tools as itself a conversation to be rehearsed. The foundational exercise contrasts two AI answers to the same investment question.

The first answer reads: "This fund is guaranteed to outperform the market because AI predicts strong growth." AUREN teaches the learner to flag it on three grounds: it makes a guarantee claim, and no investment can be guaranteed; it cites no source and offers no fundamental analysis; and its advisory tone crosses the line between education and advice — a line that matters legally and practically.

The second answer reads: "Here are factors to research: fees, diversification, risk, time horizon, official fund documents. I can't tell you whether to invest." AUREN teaches the learner to accept it — it is educational rather than advisory, it points to primary sources, and it explicitly acknowledges the AI's limits.

From this foundation, learners rehearse full dialogues with AI chatbots that hallucinate statistics, blur the education-advice boundary, or present correlation as causation. They practice the verification workflow — identify the claim type, locate the primary source, cross-check independent sources, test for advisory language, and escalate decision-grade questions to a licensed professional. The skill being built is not distrust of AI. It is calibrated trust: knowing what AI is good for, where it fails, and how to check.

**11. Problem Statement 2, part two: six rehearsal categories, one engine**

AUREN organizes its rehearsal content into six categories. Each addresses a vulnerability documented in retail-investor fraud and behavioral-finance research, and each maps to at least one of IOSCO's problem statements. All six are delivered by the same rehearsal engine — only the scenarios, AI personas, and scoring criteria change.

Deepfake and voice-clone recognition trains learners to detect AI-generated impersonations of trusted figures, with live coaching on specific technical and behavioral signals. AI tool literacy trains learners through dialogues with misleading, hallucinated, or advisory-toned chatbot content, building source-verification and escalation habits. Social media pressure trains learners inside simulated WhatsApp investment groups, TikTok finfluencer promotions, and Telegram pump-and-dump rings, building the capacity to resist group dynamics.

Emotional investing discipline trains learners through simulated market-crash panic, FOMO-driven hype cycles, and loss-aversion moments — practicing the discipline of returning to a written plan rather than reacting to fear or greed. Product comprehension trains learners through conversations in which the AI persona pushes complex products — structured notes, leveraged ETFs, crypto derivatives — and the learner practices asking the questions that reveal actual risk. And the advice-versus-education boundary trains learners to recognize the moment when general education is no longer enough and a licensed professional is required — rehearsing the escalation itself, which is the behavioral skill of knowing what you don't know.

Because the engine is category-agnostic, adding new categories, new jurisdictions, or newly emerging scam typologies does not require rebuilding the platform. The rehearsal architecture is the constant; the content library is the variable.

**Part Four: How AUREN Operates**

**12. The seven-step operating flow**

Every learner experiences the same repeatable architecture, personalized end-to-end. The flow has seven steps.

Step one is onboarding with identity verification. The learner signs up and verifies identity through integrated eKYC — passport, national ID, or QR-to-phone flow. Because identity is verified at the start, the credential this journey produces is trustworthy from day one, which matters for institutional and regulator deployments.

Step two is the Digital Human interview: a fifteen-minute natural conversation, not a questionnaire. AUREN observes reasoning patterns, AI trust levels, risk biases, emotional response indicators, and conversational tells. The learner experiences a conversation; the platform conducts an assessment.

Step three is the AILS cognitive assessment. The AI engine generates the learner's starting score across five dimensions — Investor Reasoning, AI Literacy, Risk Awareness, Scam Resistance, and Behavioral Stability — accompanied by quantitative bars, a qualitative narrative, named strengths, and specific improvement areas.

Step four is the personalized rehearsal path. Based on the AILS profile, AUREN sequences rehearsal scenarios against the learner's weakest dimensions. Weak dimensions receive more scenarios and more time; strong dimensions receive lighter coverage. Every scenario in the path is chosen to strengthen a specific, measured competency.

Step five is rehearsal with live coaching. The learner enters each scenario and converses with AI personas. Coaching prompts appear at the moment they are needed — when phrasing is risky, when a verification step is skipped, when behavioral signals of pressure or panic emerge. The coaching is corrective in real time, which is when correction actually changes behavior.

Step six is the evidential scorecard, delivered after each rehearsal. It shows quote-level feedback: what the learner said, when they said it, why it mattered, what the alternative response would have been, and how the exchange affected their competency scores.

Step seven is classification: certify, retrain, or escalate. Certified scenarios move the learner forward. Retrain classifications trigger a repeat of the scenario with variations, so the learner cannot pass by memorizing a script. Escalation flags the learner for review — self-directed remediation in consumer deployments, or human review by a supervising educator in institutional ones.

Steps five through seven loop across the personalized rehearsal path until competency is demonstrated. The flow is not a pipeline the learner passes through once; it is a training cycle that repeats until the skill is real.

**13. The evidential scorecard**

The evidential scorecard is the signature artifact of the platform, and it is easiest to understand through an example drawn from a WhatsApp scam rehearsal.

Early in the rehearsal, at turn three, the learner responds to a persona's investment pitch with: "Sure, sounds legit — how do I sign up?" AUREN's analysis flags this as a scam-vulnerability signal: immediate agreement without verification. The learner's Scam Resistance score adjusts downward by three points, and the system notes that regulator-registry lookup should be rehearsed next.

At turn five, the learner says: "I trust my friend — he wouldn't share a scam." AUREN identifies the trust-transfer bias: recruiter-driven scams specifically exploit trusted contacts, because the friend sharing the scheme is often themselves a victim being used as a distribution channel. Behavioral Stability adjusts downward by two.

Then the live coaching lands. At turn eight, the learner says: "Actually — can you send me the regulator registration first?" AUREN marks a verification signal: Scam Resistance up four points; the verification instinct is now active. And at turn eleven, the learner closes with: "I'm going to verify this offline before I do anything." The verification protocol has been correctly applied; Scam Resistance rises six points; the certification criterion for this scenario is met.

This is what quote-level scoring means. The learner is not told "you scored 42." They are shown their own words, the exact moments those words revealed vulnerability or demonstrated skill, and the specific effect on each competency dimension. The evidence is the transcript. Nothing is asserted that cannot be pointed to.

**14. Measurement: AILS as a trajectory**

The AILS score — the AI-era Investor Literacy Score — is the platform's central measurement instrument, and its defining property in v2 is that it is continuous rather than static.

A representative learner enters with a starting score of 42, placing them in the Foundation Learner band at roughly the 28th percentile of their cohort. Their dimensional breakdown shows the shape of their vulnerability: Investor Reasoning at 35, AI Literacy at 28, Risk Awareness at 45, Scam Resistance at 30, Behavioral Stability at 45. The two weakest dimensions — AI Literacy and Scam Resistance — are exactly the dimensions the AI era punishes hardest, and they drive the sequencing of this learner's rehearsal path.

As rehearsals accumulate, every session contributes evidence and the score moves. For learners with this profile who complete the personalized rehearsal program, the projected post-program score is 76 — a shift from the bottom third of the cohort to well above average, achieved not by consuming more content but by demonstrating changed behavior across repeated, varied scenarios.

Every score movement is anchored in transcript evidence. This makes AILS auditable by regulators, defensible to procurement teams, and explainable to the learner. When a regulator asks how outcomes are measured — a question IOSCO's application process asks explicitly — the answer is not a methodology description. It is the evidence trail itself.

**Part Five: Deployment and Strategic Implications**

**15. Cross-jurisdictional by design**

The rehearsal architecture is inherently jurisdiction-neutral, and AUREN localizes along two axes simultaneously.

The linguistic axis: AUREN's multilingual architecture supports English, Mandarin, Arabic, Bahasa Melayu, and Spanish, and is designed to add languages without code rebuilds. Crucially, everything localizes together — AUREN's own voice, the AI persona voices, transcripts, coaching prompts, and rehearsal scenarios. Localization goes beyond translation: a UK deepfake scenario looks different from a Brazilian pump-and-dump, and both differ from a Malaysian WhatsApp investment group. The scenarios are culturally situated, because scams are culturally situated.

The regulatory axis: compliance regime, escalation paths, certification requirements, and reporting formats are configurable per jurisdiction. UK FCA warnings, MAS typologies, SEBI alerts, and CNMV advisories all plug into the same engine. The same AUREN instance can serve a UK retail investor in English with FCA framing, a Spanish investor in Spanish with CNMV framing, and a Malaysian investor in Bahasa with Securities Commission framing.

The practical consequence: AUREN can serve IOSCO's more than 130 member jurisdictions without bespoke rebuilds. For an organization whose explicit assessment criteria include cross-jurisdictional potential at twenty percent weighting, this is not a feature. It is a qualifying condition — and AUREN meets it architecturally rather than aspirationally.

**16. The regulator view**

When AUREN runs in a jurisdiction, the deploying regulator sees four capabilities designed specifically for their oversight role.

First, anonymized cohort analytics: aggregate AILS scores and rehearsal outcomes by jurisdiction, age band, language, and risk profile, with no personal data exposed and audit-ready exports formatted for IOSCO C8 reporting. Second, vulnerability heatmaps: real-time visibility into which behavioral biases and rehearsal failure modes are most prevalent in the population, and where rehearsal libraries are producing measurable improvement.

Third, scam-detection analytics: tracking which rehearsal scenarios learners fail most often and which fraud patterns are growing. This turns AUREN into something regulators have never had — an early-warning sensor network for emerging fraud, powered by the aggregate learning behavior of the population itself. When learners across a jurisdiction suddenly begin failing a new scenario type, that signal reaches the regulator before the corresponding losses reach the complaints desk.

Fourth, cross-jurisdictional comparison: for the first time, a regulator can compare their jurisdiction's rehearsal outcomes — AI literacy, scam resistance, behavioral discipline — against anonymized peer regulators, using a common measurement standard across IOSCO members. Cross-border policy coordination on investor education has always lacked a shared instrument. AILS, deployed across jurisdictions, is that instrument.

**17. Five deployment pathways**

The rehearsal architecture creates commercial optionality far beyond a course product, along five distinct pathways.

For retail investors, AUREN operates as a business-to-consumer subscription: access to the rehearsal library, AILS assessment, and verified credential, on a freemium model with premium unlock for advanced scenarios and certification.

For banks and brokers, AUREN operates as business-to-business licensing: onboarding-integrated rehearsal for new retail customers, particularly around high-risk products, with institutional dashboards for compliance officers. The commercial logic for the institution is direct — measurably improved customer comprehension reduces mis-selling exposure.

For universities and academic institutions, AUREN provides embedded rehearsal curriculum for finance, business, and AI literacy programs, with student cohort dashboards, and a verified credential that adds market value to graduating students.

For regulators — the IOSCO membership itself — AUREN powers national public literacy programs with cross-jurisdictional cohort analytics, rehearsal libraries updated with each regulator's own scam typologies and consumer warnings, and audit-ready governance throughout.

And as a vertical extension, the same engine retuned with different scenarios and scoring criteria becomes a compliance training product: financial advisor training, compliance certification, and continuing education for the industry side of the market. The rehearsal architecture that protects retail investors also trains the professionals who serve them.

**Part Six: What We Are Asking IOSCO to See**

On Demo Day — 8 October 2026, in Madrid — AUREN will be presented to a judging panel of thirty-nine C8 Plenary members and the IOSCO Secretary General. Most submissions will be prototype education tools. What AUREN will show is different in kind, not just in degree.

AUREN will show a live rehearsal. A learner will enter a scenario in which a deepfake video is attempting to convince them to invest in a fraudulent scheme. The AI persona will apply pressure. AUREN, the Digital Human, will coach them live. The evidential scorecard will appear at the end — quote-level, evidence-based, competency-mapped. And the regulator dashboard view will show what a national deployment of this capability would look like for the very regulators sitting in the room.

The judges will not be watching a demo. They will be watching what investor education becomes when the mechanic changes — when knowledge delivery gives way to skill rehearsal, when scoring becomes evidential, and when the AI takes the role of both mentor and adversary in service of the learner's protection.

The best investor protection is not a warning label. It is a trained instinct. The AI era does not give retail investors time to look things up, verify sources, or reflect on best practices when it matters. It gives them a moment — a call that sounds exactly like their advisor, a video that looks exactly like a trusted CEO, a message from a friend that carries exactly the right social proof. AUREN trains them for that moment, before it happens, so that when it happens, they are ready.
